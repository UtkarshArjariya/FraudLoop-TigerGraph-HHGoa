## LinkedIn

I built FRAUDLOOP for the TigerGraph HHGOA Agentic Fraud Investigation hackathon.

The question I wanted to answer: can an investigator show how evidence changes its recommendation, while respecting policy and human approval?

FRAUDLOOP combines TigerGraph relationships, historical investigations, separate embedding indexes, and an explicit policy engine. Its analyst console shows the evidence, graph neighborhood, initial and final actions, report drafts, and approval routes.

The twenty saved benchmark answers validate: nine fraud judgments, eight legitimate judgments, three uncertain judgments, and two SAR recommendations. Those are system judgments, not a hidden-answer-key accuracy claim.

The biggest lessons were practical: high risk scores can mislead, common device fingerprints are weak evidence, correlated signals need caps, and replay makes a demo resilient to quota and database outages.

Additional customer replies are disclosed simulations. Card blocks and regulatory filings remain recommendations for human approval.
