## LinkedIn

Fraud alerts are easy to raise. The hard part is explaining what should happen next, and why a recommendation changes when new evidence arrives.

I built FRAUDLOOP for the TigerGraph HHGOA Agentic Fraud Investigation hackathon. A custom Python investigation loop connects transaction relationships in TigerGraph, relevant historical cases, capped evidence assessment, and explicit fraud policy. Gemini can write guarded explanations, but assessment and policy code make the decisions.

The analyst console now includes a GraphRAG inspector that shows why precedents were retrieved, human evidence requests that survive reloads, and a bounded live monitor that turns incoming transactions into reviewable cases. The timeline keeps initial and final recommendations, approval routes, and what changed in view.

In saved HHG-002, a disclosed simulated customer confirmation changes the recommendation from verification to closing the alert as legitimate. The simulation demonstrates the workflow; it is not proof of a real customer response.

All 20 required answer files pass the project's validation checks: nine fraud, eight legitimate, three uncertain judgments, and two SAR recommendations. Those are system outputs, not an accuracy score against the hidden answer key.

Card blocks and SAR filings remain recommendations for human approval. The prototype does not execute bank actions.
